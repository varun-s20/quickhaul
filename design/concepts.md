# Quick Haul: three concepts (phase A)
Art Director, 2026-11-03. Primary reference: Truewerk (references/deconstruct/truewerk.md). Section donors: FREITAG, Muval and Piece of Cake. Varun did not name their sections, so I scoped each one to the part the scout proposed (see "Donor scope" below). The type and palette options are in prototype/audition.html.

What every concept starts from:
- The client's only asset is assets/logo.png: chrome on a slate wall, close to monochrome (hue 219, very low saturation). There are no photos or video yet, and Varun's checkpoint 1a rules out stock. So no concept can lean on Varun's V5 full-screen client video. Anti-slop says missing media gets a custom illustration, never a placeholder, so each concept carries its first screen with type, the logo's colours and Truewerk's graphic devices.
- Truewerk's devices are all baked into one flat hero image on its own site. We take the techniques, not the art, and rebuild each one live from the measurements: the hazard-stripe edge (25px wide at 1440, top 52% of the hero, bars 29 degrees off horizontal, 47 on and 13 off), the off-white slats cut into the hero's bottom edge (29px tall, 164px wide, 210.6px pitch, from 43% across), the 40px gradient halftone band (5.5px staggered pitch, 3.1px dots thinning downward), the speck grain, the hairline registration rules, heavy caps against light caps, and mono for facts. All of them come across in the logo's slate and chrome, not Truewerk's lime.
- The header keeps Truewerk's structure (a slim dark mono bar over a sticky header) but must stay quiet: the tallest piece under about 90px at 1440 by 900, against Truewerk's 145px of chrome. The bar is where Shawn's own line lives on every page: "Need help booking or have questions? Give us a call" with the number.
- The logo goes in as a tile, FREITAG's way (references/deconstruct/freitag.md): FREITAG's logo is a flat 144 by 48 tile that fills its 48px bar top to bottom and sits over the bar's rules like a tab, so it never needs a cutout. Ours is a square tile cropped from Shawn's own slate wall around the chrome mark, which solves the logo that is not transparent. FREITAG's 0.8px ruled sheet (a 12-column grid built from cell borders, no gutters) is the model for concept 1's ticket and for the Donate page.

## 1. The work order
Big idea: every Quick Haul job is a ticket with four facts on it: the town, how much junk, the day and the price. The site is that ticket, and the visitor fills it in by tapping. Truewerk reads like a spec sheet for gear; here the same mono labels, hairline rules and hazard edge read as a dispatch sheet for a crew. In the first five seconds the visitor should feel "these guys are organised, they come to my town, and I will know roughly what it costs before anyone shows up." The hero is a slate field on Truewerk's banner grid: the two-row caps headline sits left, with the chrome hazard edge cut into the left margin and the slats biting into the bottom edge. Under the headline runs a ruled ticket strip of four fields: Town (type it and the field answers that we come to you, or "Can't see your town? Call us"), Load (four chips for an eighth, a quarter, half and a full truck, each with a small truck-bed fill mark, rebuilt from Muval's "What are you moving?" selector), Day (the next open days), and a "from" price that changes with the load. The ticket ends in the Book now pill with the call line beside it, Piece of Cake's quote-plus-call pairing in Shawn's words. It answers the proposal's three questions in the promised order: where, roughly what it costs, how to book. The halftone band under the hero becomes the tear-off edge of the ticket. Risk: it runs on placeholder towns and prices until Shawn confirms, and a form in the hero turns into a software widget if the ruling and type do not carry it.

## 2. Space back
Big idea: people are not buying hauling, they are buying the empty garage afterwards ("freeing space" is Shawn's own phrase for his customers). The whole site is one clear-out: each section takes something away, and the page gets emptier and lighter as you go down. In the first five seconds the visitor should feel relief: "that is my garage, and it can be empty." The hero is a custom illustration, since we have no photos: a packed garage drawn in Truewerk's halftone, in chrome dots on slate (a couch on end, a mattress, a fridge, stacked boxes, yard bags). Truewerk's slats become the panels of a roll-up garage door that lifts on load over a long ease-out. Then, as the visitor scrolls, the items are carried out of frame one by one toward the right, the way the truck in the logo faces, until only the floor line, the headline and Book now are left. With reduced motion the door is open and the garage is already clear. Further down, the same halftone grade becomes the house treatment for Shawn's before-and-after phone photos, so the drawing and his real jobs look like one family. Risk: the illustration is real drawing work and must not look like clip art; the scroll sequence must read at 390; and it is the most expensive concept to build inside the budget.

## 3. Round trip
Big idea: the logo already says it. The arrows run in a ring around the truck, so junk does not just vanish, it goes round: donate first, recycle next, dump last. The site follows one load from your garage to where it ends up, which also gives the Donate page Shawn asked for a reason to sit in the main story. In the first five seconds the visitor should feel trust: "a local crew that does this properly." The hero sets the two-row headline left on slate, and on the right a large hairline ring in chrome, the logo's arrow ring redrawn as a single 1px line with three arrowheads (a glyph taken from the logo, the way Acrylic Pros reused its fin, not a new logo), bleeding off the right edge. Three mono stops sit on the ring: Donate, Recycle, Dispose. The hazard edge marks the stop you are at. As the visitor scrolls, the ring turns a third of a turn per chapter and the next stop comes to the top; with reduced motion it stays still. Book now and the call line sit under the headline. Risk: where Quick Haul's items actually go is not confirmed (brief, open question 5), so the three stops are claims we cannot make until Shawn says which charities and recyclers he uses. A big ring with nothing true on it would be decoration.

## My recommendation
Concept 1. It does the job the brief sets (town, price, booking, call, in that order), it is the most natural rebuild of Truewerk's spec-sheet system, and it does not depend on drawing we have not made or facts we do not have. Concept 2 is the most original and the one I would most like to see, but it costs the most to build and lives or dies on the illustration. Concept 3 is the most specific to this logo, but it needs Shawn's donation facts first.

## Recommended pairing and palette, for the record
- Type: B, Barlow with Roboto Mono for facts only. Both come from the reference, not from habit: Roboto Mono is Truewerk's own label and button face, and Barlow is declared in Truewerk's own stylesheet, though not loaded on the pages measured (references/deconstruct/truewerk.md, Type). That answers the anti-slop "generic typography" line, which names Roboto. A (Bio Sans) has no open licence and its web licence terms were not checked. C (Archivo expanded) echoes the wordmark but loses it on phones, where it has to drop to normal width.
- Palette: 1, Slate wall. It is the logo as Shawn already shows it. Palette 3 is the choice if Varun wants one colour accent; its steel blue is derived from the logo's hue, not a literal pixel.

## Conflicts for Varun (resolved by the art-direct order unless he says otherwise)
1. Buttons: Truewerk uses square blocks and zero radius everywhere. Varun's V9 pills win on buttons. That leaves pills on a square system, so I propose pills for buttons and chips only, and square corners on every frame, tile and image.
2. Accent colour: Truewerk's lime is a big part of its punch. V6 builds the palette from the logo, which has no accent, so the stripe and highlights come across in chrome (or steel blue in palette 3). The site will be quieter than Truewerk.
3. Hero media: V5 wants full-screen looping footage of the client's work. None exists, so the hero is type-led (concepts 1 and 3) or a custom illustration (concept 2). When Shawn's photos arrive they go in below the hero first, under one grade.
4. Hero height: Truewerk's hero is a 515px banner under 145px of sticky chrome. V5 is full screen. The primary wins on layout, so my default is Truewerk's banner proportion with the halftone band and the next section showing on the first screen. Concept 2 needs the full screen, so choosing it means overruling this.
5. Header: Truewerk's bar plus header is 145px. The quiet-nav rule caps the tallest piece near 90px, and Varun's floating three-piece header (V4, "V+R, unsplit") is allowed only where the reference supports it, which Truewerk does not. Default: Truewerk's sticky bar and header, slimmed, with the call line in the bar.
6. Never taken from Truewerk: the em dash after every heading, full-stop headlines, and two colours inside one headline (V1 and anti-slop). Mono body copy moves to labels only in pairing B, for older readers on phones.

## Donor scope (my assumption, for Varun to confirm)
Varun named the donors but not their sections. Following the scout's cards, all three are now measured: FREITAG for the header (pills, boxed logo, icon circles) and its mission block as the Donate page donor (references/deconstruct/freitag.md); Muval for the "What are you moving?" selector, the per-town price cards and the header phone with the 390 drawer (muval.md); Piece of Cake for the hero quote-plus-call block, the phone-with-hours header, the review-score row and the 390 date-first form (pieceofcake.md). If Varun meant other sections, those deconstructions need a rerun before phase B.

What the donor measurements change in the concepts:
- Concept 1's ticket takes Muval's placement: one bar that straddles the hero's bottom edge (on Muval, 50px inside the photo and 42px below it, with the next section pulled up under it), so the ticket reads as the hinge between hero and page. It becomes a square ruled strip, not Muval's 150px-radius pill, to fit Truewerk's square frames. Muval's chips are plain links with no selected state; ours must be real radio controls with a visible selected and focus state, because the price has to change with the load. At 390 Muval goes to a 2 by 2 chip grid of 163px chips and "A few rooms" wraps; our four labels (1/8 truck, 1/4 truck, 1/2 truck, Full truck) fit that width on one line.
- The call line takes Piece of Cake's weighting: the button and a text-only call link of near-equal width, side by side, the call link centred on the button and the pair going full width and stacked at 390. Its hours trick (a small line hung under the phone that takes no header height) suits the quiet-nav cap and goes in the top bar. Not inherited: its missing focus ring, its 20 by 20 mobile phone target, and a call option that opens a page instead of tel:.
- Piece of Cake's review row (logo, score and count, no stars, no card; 2 by 2 at 390) is the model for the Facebook rating line, which stays hidden until Shawn's real rating exists (brief: no unearned rating).
- Tells inside the donor sections that we do not take: Piece of Cake's full-stop H1, two-colour subline and accent-coloured words in a headline; Muval's full-stop headline with an orange full stop. Muval had no reduced-motion override; ours will.

## What I could not check or had to guess
- No hero media exists, so the palettes are shown on the one real frame we have, the logo mockup. Contrast over future photos is untested.
- The logo is a 736 by 1600 phone screenshot with the mark at 220 by 233 pixels on a wall that is not transparent. It stays sharp only up to about 110px wide on 2x screens. The original 2.6 MB PNG from Fiverr, or a vector, is needed before phase B.
- Bio Sans: commercial (Dharma Type, sold on MyFonts from $29.99 a style, full family $199.99; rechecked by search on 2026-11-03). I did not read its web licence terms, and I did not render it, because we never load a reference's font files.
- The audition loads Barlow, Roboto Mono and Archivo from Google Fonts. Phase B self-hosts whichever is chosen.
- Every fact on the audition page (phone, prices, towns) is a placeholder under the checkpoint 1a rules.
- The 390 checks ran in desktop Chromium, not touch emulation. Motion is described, not built.
