Still good? Someone wants it

A lot of what we pick up still works, so the good stuff goes to local charities and reuse stores instead of the dump.

What we can donate

The dresser that doesn't fit the new place, the kids' bikes, the spare couch from the basement. If you'd use it, someone else probably will too.

{{picture name="dresser-on-street" class="o-donateBand__img" alt="A small wooden dresser set out on an empty street, in front of a wooden fence" sizes="(min-width: 1025px) max(46vw, 800px), 100vw"}}

Yes to furniture in good shape, working appliances, kitchenware, clothes and shoes, books, toys, bikes, tools and sports gear.

Not these

  • Anything broken, stained or missing parts
  • Worn-out mattresses
  • Car seats
  • Recalled cribs
  • Anything that's been in a flood or fire

Good to know Massachusetts bans clothes and other textiles from the regular trash.

How it works

Add a note when you book saying what's still good, or tell us when we get there. We'll load it separately.

  • Where it goes Local charities and reuse stores in Massachusetts and New Hampshire. We check what they're taking before each drop-off.
  • If it can't be donated It goes with the rest of your load, so you're not stuck with it.
  • Need a receipt? Say so when you book and we'll ask the charity for one.

Getting it ready

Charities are more likely to say yes to things that are clean and complete.

  • Empty it Check drawers, pockets and boxes, and take out anything personal.
  • Keep it together Tape screws, shelves and cords to the piece they belong to.
  • Wipe it down A quick clean goes a long way.

Run a charity nearby?

If you run a shelter, a thrift store or a reuse store in Massachusetts or New Hampshire, tell us what you can use.
