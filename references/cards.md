# Quick Haul: reference cards
Scout, 2026-11-03. I visited each finalist myself at 1440x900 and 390x844 (desktop Chromium, scrollbar hidden, no touch emulation). I read fonts from document.fonts and computed styles. Licence status comes from what I know of each foundry; I did not check foundry pages this run. Only six sites passed screening, so there are six cards, not the 8 to 10 the procedure asks for. I did not promote any killed site to make up the number.

Brief reminders that shape every card: the client has a chrome-and-slate logo (assets/logo.png: a circular arrow ring around a loaded dump truck, heavy italic QUICK HAUL wordmark, a bin at the base, shown on a slate wall mockup), no photos yet, and later only phone photos of real jobs. The palette comes from the logo, so any accent colour seen below is the reference's, not ours.

## 1. FREITAG (https://www.freitag.ch)
- Found via: cross-niche (analogy 5, reuse and circular), search 55
- Role: primary candidate (visual system); also section donor for the Donate page
- What it is: a Swiss maker of bags cut from used truck tarps. The site is a shop with a strong mission and circular-services side.
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: partial. It has category tiles (maps to services), a "NEAR YOU" store finder (maps to service area) and impact statements (maps to Donate). It has no booking, phone or price-first hero, so those must come from donors.
  - Media: good. The impact comes from type, hairline rules and framing, not the photos. Its tiles would hold imperfect phone photos well, because every image sits in the same ruled frame.
  - Mood: clean, dependable, a bit industrial. "Bold" comes from size, not colour.
- What to take: a page ruled like a worksheet, with hairline frames on the outer edge and between tiles; oversized regular-weight grotesque caps for section statements; small mono labels with a square bullet (sparingly, only where they add a fact, per Varun's eyebrow rule); grey pill links with arrows; a header of pills on the left, a boxed logo in the centre and icon circles on the right. Varun's own header and pill-button moves (signature 4 and 9) fit straight onto this.
- How the client slots in: Quick Haul's chrome logo in the centre box on slate, with the palette from the logo (slate, near-black, silver-white). The ruled grid becomes a load ticket or dispatch sheet: mono labels carry real facts (towns, truck-load sizes, "from" prices). Phone photos go in the ruled tiles. Booking and Call pills sit on the right.
- Risks: it is a shop with fashion photography in the hero, so the hero must be rebuilt as type-first. It has no conversion structure, so Muval and Piece of Cake have to supply it. The screener saw two tells: mono eyebrow labels and a row of three cards. Heavy use of the mono labels would become the AI eyebrow, so they need rationing. Hover states were subtle; no visible change was captured on the pill. Its fonts are commercial.
- Fonts: Akkurat Std (AkkStdRg): commercial (Lineto). Akkurat Mono (AkkStdMn): commercial (Lineto). "FRg": unknown, possibly a custom brand face. DM Sans, Inter and Open Sans also load: free, and they appear to come from third-party widgets.
- Anti-slop: mostly clean. Mono eyebrow labels on section intros, and one three-card row. No italics, no full-stop headlines seen, no glow CTAs.
- Estimated design quality and originality (rubric scale): design 8, originality 8.

## 2. Muval (https://www.muval.com.au)
- Found via: cross-niche (analogy 2, modern DTC movers), Zarla moving roundup
- Role: primary candidate (structure); also section donor for the estimate selector and the service-area price cards
- What it is: an Australian platform to compare and book removalists, with instant quotes.
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: the best structural match on the list. The nav carries "Call: 1300 ..." next to an outlined "Get instant quotes" button. The hero has a "What are you moving?" selector (Whole home, A few rooms, A few items, Something else) that leads to "Get prices". Below it, per-city price cards show "$79/half hour" style averages. At 390 the drawer pins the phone and "Get instant quotes" at the bottom, in thumb reach.
  - Media: weak as built (a stock lifestyle hero, stock city photos on the cards, a stock model on the testimonial). The system itself is white cards, one orange and a rounded sans, and it would carry type-only cards.
  - Mood: quick and clean, friendly rather than bold.
- What to take: the selector pattern, rebuilt as "How much junk?" (an eighth, a quarter, half, a full truck) leading to "See my price". The per-town price cards become a service-area band: each town with a "from" price and no photo. Also the phone-plus-primary pairing in the header and drawer, and the chip hover (dark outline, light fill).
- How the client slots in: the selector chips become truck-load fractions with a small truck glyph drawn from the logo. The cards list MA and NH towns with placeholder prices (per Varun's checkpoint 1a). Orange becomes the logo's own accent, or slate on silver.
- Risks: it is a platform, not a crew, so it has no "who we are" warmth. Swap every stock image. One tell: a full-stop headline with an orange full stop. The testimonial uses an em dash before the name. At 390 the phone number drops out of the header bar and only appears inside the drawer. The font is commercial.
- Fonts: GT Haptik: commercial (Grilli Type).
- Anti-slop: one design tell (full-stop headline). One copy tell (em dash before the testimonial name).
- Estimated design quality and originality (rubric scale): design 6, originality 5.
- Screens: screens/muval-first.png (1440, by screener), screens/muval-cityprices-1440.png, screens/muval-first-390.png, screens/muval-menu-390.png, screens/muval-launcher-default.png, screens/muval-launcher-hover.png

## 3. Piece of Cake Moving & Storage (https://mypieceofcakemove.com)
- Found via: niche search and roundups (adjacent business: local movers), Zarla moving roundup
- Role: primary candidate (local-crew structure); also section donor for the hero quote-plus-call block and the review-score row
- What it is: a New York moving and storage company with a one-colour brand (hot pink and navy). No MA, NH or junk links were found on the homepage.
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: strong on the first screen. The header has "Instant price" and the phone number with "7 days a week 7AM-9PM" under it. The hero has a two-field quote form, "Get quote" and "Or get a call from us" side by side, which is almost exactly Shawn's "need help booking? give us a call". Under the hero sits a review-score row across Google, Yelp, Trustpilot and Thumbtack. At 390 the hero becomes a date-first booking form (move date, type, from, to), close to Quick Haul's calendar booking. The mobile menu ends with a phone pill and a Get quote pill plus the hours.
  - Media: weak for the hero, which is a branded studio shoot with models and pink bins. The crew-with-truck photo further down is the kind of honest job photo Quick Haul will have.
  - Mood: bold and quick, louder than "clean".
- What to take: the quote-plus-call pairing in the hero; phone with hours in the header; the review-score row (as a Facebook rating with a link out to verify); the date-first mobile form; the "what's included" checklist (maps to "What we take").
- How the client slots in: the colour wall becomes slate from the logo, with the chrome logo on it. The quote form becomes "Pick a day" plus "How much junk?". The review row shows Facebook only, linked, never with an unearned rating.
- Risks: it is built on Avada (WordPress), and below the hero it reads as a theme: heading-left/body-right sections, headlines with an accent-coloured word, and sentence headlines ending in full stops. Montserrat is a default-feeling face. An accessibility overlay widget is fixed on the left. Its hover is a colour tint only. Take the first screen and the review row, not the rest.
- Fonts: Montserrat 400 to 800: free (OFL).
- Anti-slop: tells seen. Full-stop sentence headlines, accent-coloured words inside headlines ("flat fee"), and heading-left/body-right in the pricing section. The screener counted one tell on the first screen; my deeper scroll found more.
- Estimated design quality and originality (rubric scale): design 6, originality 5.
- Screens: screens/pieceofcake-first.png (1440, by screener), screens/pieceofcake-hero-1440.png, screens/pieceofcake-pricing-1440.png, screens/pieceofcake-first-390.png, screens/pieceofcake-menu-390.png, screens/pieceofcake-hover-quote.png

- Found via: cross-niche (analogy 3, workwear), Scout's knowledge
- Role: section donor for section dividers and the hero accent system
- What it is: a US workwear brand (e-commerce).
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: none relevant (it is a store).
  - Media: the hero is polished photography, but the parts worth taking are graphic: a hazard-stripe edge on the hero, a halftone dot band as a section divider, mono labels, and heavy caps.
  - Mood: bold, hard-wearing, job-site.

The 390 horizontal mono category row could become a services quick-links row. The hexagon close button in the menu is a nice small touch.

Copy habits: em dashes at the end of headings (after "BUILT LIKE IT MATTERS" and "MOST POPULAR") and full-stop headlines. The two hero lines in different colours count as a headline colour change. A chat widget overlaps content. Hover was not captured.
- Fonts: BioSans 300 and 900: unknown licence. Roboto Mono: free (Apache 2.0). Roboto: free.
- Anti-slop: em dashes in headings (copy tell), full-stop headlines, and a colour change inside a headline.
- Estimated design quality and originality (rubric scale): design 7, originality 6.

## 5. Furniture Bank (https://www.furniturebank.org)
- Found via: cross-niche (analogy 5, reuse and circular), search 34
- Role: section donor for the Donate page
- What it is: a Toronto charity that runs a paid furniture-removal service and gives the furniture to families. In effect it is a hauler with a donation story, which is close to the Quick Haul Donate angle.
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure (Donate): strong. The hero pairs "The professional removal service you'd expect" with "the community impact you wouldn't". "Request a pickup" sits in the top bar. A "proof is in the numbers" block (trucks, team, cycle time, families helped) has two pickup buttons, and there is a review count.
  - Media: mixed. A cut-out truck photo and stats in a colour block work without good photography; lower sections lean on photos.
  - Mood: warm, community, less bold.
- What to take: the two-part promise as the Donate page's opening idea, rewritten in Shawn's voice; the numbers block as a Donate impact band (placeholder figures, flagged must-confirm); a pickup CTA inside the impact block.
- How the client slots in: Quick Haul's impact numbers (loads diverted, items donated) are unknown, so they stay placeholders and must be confirmed before launch. The truck cut-out idea works with a phone photo of Shawn's own truck.
- Risks: built in Elementor and fairly generic. Poppins. A hand-drawn underline flourish under the headline. A fixed "Donate" side tab overlaps the mobile menu at 390 (the confirmed floating-widget issue). A cookie banner and a donation toast cover content (I did not accept tracking). The headline wraps unevenly at 390.
- Fonts: Poppins 400 to 800: free (OFL).
- Anti-slop: one three-card row; otherwise template-plain rather than slop.
- Estimated design quality and originality (rubric scale): design 5, originality 5.
- Screens: screens/furniturebank-first.png (1440, by screener), screens/furniturebank-stats-1440.png, screens/furniturebank-first-390.png, screens/furniturebank-menu-390.png

## 6. Stuf Storage (https://www.stufstorage.com)
- Found via: niche search and roundups (adjacent: storage), Scout's knowledge, checked by WebFetch
- Role: section donor for the hero
- What it is: a US self-storage startup (storage, not junk removal; it has a Boston location, which does not trigger the exclusion).
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure (hero): good. A saturated colour field, a big headline, "No hidden fees. Fast booking." as the subline, a city-or-zip search with a full-width "Find storage near me" button, and a phone icon in the 390 header. This answers "do you come to my town" and "how do I book" in one block.
  - Media: good for the hero, which is type and colour only, with a band of geometric colour shapes under it. Below the hero it turns generic (stock city photos, location cards, a press-logo wall).
  - Mood: clean and quick.
- What to take: the one-colour hero field with a town search and one big button; the phone icon kept in the mobile header bar; the trust line above the headline (as "Rated on Facebook" once real reviews exist).
- How the client slots in: a slate field, the chrome logo, "Do we come to you?" with a town field that checks the service list and offers "Can't see your town? Call us".
- Risks: the serif headline (Source Serif) is the wrong voice for Quick Haul. There is a promo bar, and a chat widget overlaps content at 390. I did not open the mobile menu (I could not find its toggle as a button). I did not open the Size Guide.
- Fonts: Source Serif Pro: free (OFL). GT America: commercial (Grilli Type).
- Anti-slop: below-hero card grids are generic; the hero is clean.
- Estimated design quality and originality (rubric scale): design 6, originality 5.
